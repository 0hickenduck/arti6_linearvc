# Field Map 2024-2026: Singing Voice Conversion, Style Conversion, Speech/Singing Conversion, and Representation Factorization

Date: 2026-06-08.

## Executive Read

The field has moved from "make singer A sound like singer B" toward controlled
singing style conversion, zero-shot robustness, and representation factorization.
SVCC 2025 is the cleanest signal: top systems can now reach very strong singer
identity similarity, sometimes comparable to ground-truth references, but
naturalness and style similarity remain difficult, especially for dynamic
techniques such as breathy voice, glissando, and vibrato.

For the current `leakage-multiscale-disentanglement` task, the defensible
research gap is not "another SVC model." The stronger thesis lane is a
controlled representation/intervention study: where speaker identity, vocal
mode, content, F0, and technique leak across frozen SSL or codec streams, and
whether a small learned residual improves an open baseline such as Seed-VC or
FACodec/Seed-VC conditioning on unseen singers.

Open-source code is the practical gate. The immediately benchmarkable systems
are GTSinger, Seed-VC, FACodec, FreeSVC, Serenade, Vevo/Amphion inference, and
HQ-SVC inference. S2Voice, DAFMSVC, R2-SVC, and singing-to-speech flow are
important references, but default to survey/reference-only until official code
is usable.

## Top Venues, Challenges, and Labs

- SVCC/VCC: the most relevant controlled challenge line. SVCC 2025 explicitly
  introduced singing style conversion with in-domain and zero-shot tracks, seven
  styles, subjective MOS/AB/XAB tests, and open baselines.
- ICASSP/Interspeech: strongest concentration of current SVC/SSC systems,
  including FreeSVC, S2Voice, DAFMSVC, SingMOS-Pro, and the SVCC 2025 paper.
- NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks: important for dataset work; GTSinger is the
  central 2024 singing corpus anchor.
- AAAI/TASLP/EUSIPCO: relevant for high-quality SVC and unified speech/singing
  generation; HQ-SVC, Vevo2, and Serenade land here.
- Active groups: Nagoya/Toda lab around SVCC and Serenade; CUHK-Shenzhen/Zhizheng
  Wu and Amphion/Vevo; Zhejiang for GTSinger; BUPT for HQ-SVC; ASLP@NPU for
  S2Voice; Kyoto/Tsinghua-related SVC work appears in Interspeech/ICME threads.

## What Strong Results Mean Now

- Singing style conversion: SVCC-style subjective tests matter most. Strong
  systems win on naturalness, singer identity similarity, and style similarity,
  not only one axis.
- Singer identity: top SVCC systems can be very strong, so identity conversion
  alone is less novel unless tested zero-shot, cross-mode, robustly, or with
  explicit leakage controls.
- Style similarity: still unsolved. The hard cases are dynamic and phonation-like
  effects, especially breathy, glissando, and vibrato.
- Objective metrics: useful for triage, not enough for claims. The SVCC analysis
  reports that objective metrics such as chroma alignment and speaker-embedding
  non-match metrics correlate with subjective scores better than many others,
  but still cannot replace listening tests.
- Resource claims: HQ-SVC makes low-resource SVC central. For a master's project,
  the right bar is not to train a giant foundation model; it is to beat or
  explain strong open baselines under a reproducible, small-GPU protocol.

## Hot Problems

- Fine-grained singing style conversion: the field is actively moving toward
  breathiness, vibrato, glissando, falsetto, mixed voice, pharyngeal placement,
  and other technique/style controls rather than only target singer identity.
- Zero-shot and low-resource SVC: open systems increasingly emphasize target
  references, few/no target training samples, and small inference/training cost.
- Real-world robustness: separated vocals, background leakage, reverberation,
  pitch errors, and web-harvested data are becoming first-class issues.
- Representation factorization: content, prosody, timbre, style, residual
  acoustic detail, and speaker identity are being encoded through SSL features,
  codec streams, VQ bottlenecks, or diffusion/flow conditioning.
- Objective metric calibration: subjective listening is expensive; SingMOS-Pro
  and SVCC metric-correlation work show high demand for better automatic judges.
- Speech/singing bridge tasks: STS and S2S are valuable for understanding mode
  shifts, phonetic preservation, and prompt normalization.

## Saturated or Risky Claims

- Plain clean-data singer-identity SVC is crowded and near-saturated as a thesis
  contribution unless the paper adds open evaluation, robustness, or controlled
  disentanglement.
- A factorized codec alone is not novel now. NaturalSpeech 3/FACodec and Vevo
  already establish strong factorization narratives.
- Objective metrics alone are not enough for singing style claims. SVCC shows
  correlation but not replacement for subjective evaluation.
- GTSinger cannot support independent language-disentanglement claims because
  singer and language are confounded. Use FreeSVC or another multilingual source
  for cross-lingual stress tests, not causal language claims.
- Training a new large SVC/SSC model is a poor fit for a small-GPU master's
  project unless the main contribution is data/evaluation or a tiny adapter over
  frozen representations.

## Code and Checkpoint Availability

- Strongly usable: GTSinger, Seed-VC, FACodec, FreeSVC, Serenade.
- Usable with caveats: Vevo1.5/Vevo2 via Amphion/Hugging Face for inference;
  full data/reproduction is limited by internal SingNet use. HQ-SVC has
  inference code and pretrained models, but training code was not released in
  the checked README.
- Reference only by default: S2Voice, DAFMSVC, R2-SVC, singing-to-speech flow.
- Dataset/reference: SingMOS-Pro is public and useful for evaluator calibration,
  but it is not a conversion system baseline.

## Recommended Master's-Scale Directions

1. Frozen leakage cube plus small intervention.
   Use GTSinger, WavLM/Whisper/ContentVec, and FACodec. Probe speaker, vocal
   mode, technique, F0, energy, and phones across layers and temporal bands.
   Then test whether a learned speech-to-singing residual improves cross-mode
   speaker verification or Seed-VC conditioning on held-out singers.

2. Style leakage and controllability audit.
   Use GTSinger technique labels and SVCC-style splits. Compare raw SSL,
   FACodec streams, and Seed-VC/Serenade conditioning for technique leakage,
   identity preservation, and melody preservation.

3. Objective metrics versus probe-based diagnostics.
   Reproduce SVCC-inspired objective metrics and compare them against probe
   leakage and SingMOS-Pro-style quality predictors. The contribution would be a
   calibrated evaluation protocol, not another generator.

4. Cross-lingual stress test without overclaiming.
   Use FreeSVC and multilingual speech/singing datasets to test whether content
   and speaker encoders leak language. Keep claims to observed model behavior,
   not same-person language identity unless the dataset controls it.

5. Robustness extension after clean Stage A.
   Add R2-SVC-style separated-vocal artifacts, reverb/echo, and F0 perturbations
   to the best frozen-feature setup. This is valuable only after clean-mode
   leakage behavior is understood.
